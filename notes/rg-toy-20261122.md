# Recursive generalization with RL from scratch: toy world and arm sim (2026-11-22, overnight)

Status: toy complete (8 rounds, 5 to 10 seeds per recipe). Arm sim: reaching works, lifting does not yet; one run
still going when this was written. Simulation only; the toy has no arm and no camera.

## Question

The feature probes and the stacking/order tranche (`notes/recursive-generalization-20261119.md`) found that features
stack and combine but that order did not matter, for two features that do not need each other. The user's plan for
2026-11-22 is to train a policy from scratch with RL on a simple task using the recursive-generalization idea. So,
before spending a day on it in the arm sim:

1. Does a staged curriculum make from-scratch RL work where flat RL fails, and what does it need?
2. Does a skill built on lower skills inherit how far the lower skills generalize? (That is the "recursive" part.)
3. What does from-scratch RL cost in the arm sim?

## Toy world

`tools/rg_toy.py`. A point gripper in a unit box moves a cube onto a target; PPO from scratch, 2-layer MLP,
256 environments, about 16,000 steps per second per core, 6 to 7 million steps per run.

- Inputs are absolute positions, so going somewhere never visited is something the policy must have learned.
- Stages, each the previous one plus more: `over` (low over the cube), `lift`, `carry` (held cube over the target),
  `place` (starts holding the cube; put it on the target and let go), `full` (everything, success-only reward).
- No task label in the policy's input. The critic is told the task from round 2 on.
- Regions: `L` is the left half (x < 0.5), `R` the right half. Evaluation: mean action, 1000 episodes per cell.

## Result 1: what a staged curriculum needs

Full-task success, whole workspace, mean over seeds (seeds that learned it / seeds):

| Round | Recipe | Full task | What it showed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | flat, success-only reward | 0.00 (0/5) | the task is not solvable by exploration alone |
| 1 | flat, hand-shaped reward | 0.84 (3/5) | shaping alone works, not on every seed |
| 1 | stages reach / lift / place, any order or mix | 0.00 to 0.17 | the stages contradicted each other: reach forbids closing, lift punishes the release that place needs; only lift survived |
| 2 | prefix stages (over, lift, carry), then success-only | 0.00 (0/5) | stages learned (1.00 / 1.00 / 1.00 when mixed) but never chained |
| 2 | same, no replay | 0.00 | earlier stages forgotten (carry 0.03) |
| 3 | prefix stages, then the shaped full task | 0.00 | a shaped last stage did not help either |
| 4 | bounded action mean (tanh) | flat shaped 1.00 (5/5, 3.0 to 3.8 M steps); staged 1/5 | unbounded outputs were part of it: flat RL became reliable |
| 5 | + `place` as its own stage | replay 0/5, no replay 2/5 | replay made chaining worse |
| 6 | + advantages normalised per task | 1/5 | not the cause |
| 7 | + L2 0.01 on the value before the tanh | no replay 4/5 near 1.00; replay 0/5 | see the trace below |
| 8 | + old stages replayed success-only | 5/10 near 1.00 (mean 0.59) | replay and chaining together |

The trace that explained rounds 2 to 6 (one failing seed, 2000 episodes): the policy picked up, lifted and carried the
cube to the target in 100% of episodes and then held it there, gripper output at exactly 1.00, 0% releases. The
`place` stage itself had never been learned.

Four conditions, each necessary in this toy:

1. **Stages must be prefixes of the final task.** If the best action in a state differs between two stages, and the
   policy cannot tell the stages apart, only one survives.
2. **Every new step needs its own stage**, practised from the states the stage before it ends in. Success-only reward
   on the whole task did not discover the last step after the stages.
3. **A finished skill must stay adjustable.** "Gripper closed" was right in every state of every early stage, so that
   output saturated and its gradient vanished. A bounded mean plus a small penalty on the pre-squash value fixed it.
   Flat shaped RL also went from 3/5 to 5/5 seeds.
4. **Replay old stages without their shaping.** Shaped stages punish letting go anywhere but exactly on the target;
   replayed beside the last stage they swamp its success-only signal. Replayed success-only they keep the old skills
   (0.97 to 0.98) and let the new one in.

What did not hold: the staged ladder is not better than a good hand-shaped reward on the whole task. Flat shaped RL
reaches 1.00 on 5/5 seeds in about 3 M steps; the best ladder reaches it on 5/10 seeds in 5 to 7 M. In this toy a
ladder is worth it only for what result 2 shows.

## Result 2: inherited generalization

Round 8 recipe, 10 seeds. Low stages (`over`, `lift`) trained on the whole workspace or on L only; high stages
(`carry`, `place`, `full`) trained on L only in both. Means over the seeds that learned the full task on L (9 of 10 in
each staged column), flat column 5 seeds:

| Full task tested on | Low stages everywhere | Low stages on L only | Flat shaped RL on L only |
|---|---|---|---|
| L (trained) | 1.00 | 0.97 | 1.00 |
| pick in R, place in L | **0.96** | 0.35 | 0.41 |
| pick in L, place in R | **0.82** | 0.25 | 0.44 |
| both in R | **0.78** | 0.48 | 0.52 |

- The full task was never trained with a cube in R, and it picks there at 0.96: it inherits the reach of `lift`.
- Carrying and placing were never trained in R at all, and placing into R still rises from 0.25 to 0.82. Knowing how
  to move the gripper in R (from `over` and `lift`) carried over to moving it there while holding the cube.
- One seed of 10 in each staged column never learned the full task (0.01 to 0.17 on L); it is excluded above.

This is the result that separates a ladder from flat shaped RL: the flat policy trained on L is as good on L and
generalizes no better than chance extrapolation (0.41 to 0.52). The expensive stage can be trained narrowly if the
cheap stages below it were trained widely.

## Result 3: the arm sim

`tools/rg_arm.py`: PPO from scratch on the nominal SO-101 scene, state inputs (joints, jaw point, cube, square; no
camera), the certified command gate and physics, 7200 training placements, evaluation on 96 of the 100 held-out probe
placements. One seed each. About 340 steps per second per core alone; 350 to 600 steps per second per run as run here.

| Run | Result on held-out placements |
|---|---|
| reach (jaw point to 45 mm above the cube, cube not pushed) | 0.80 at 1.0 M steps, 0.96 at 2.0 M |
| v1: lift after reach, and lift flat | 0.00 through 5 M: the gripper starts nearly closed (0.23) and has to open to 0.86 before it descends; the reward did not say so |
| v2 staged with shaped replay (open and hover, descend, grasp, lift) | hover 0.96, descend 0.72, grasp 0.23 (best), lift 0.00 at 6.0 M |
| v2 flat, same reward | hover up to 0.28, descend 0.07, grasp 0.06 to 0.11, lift 0.00 at 6.0 M |
| v3 staged, no replay, unsaturated outputs | hover 0.89 at 1.5 M; running |

- The ladder gets further than flat RL on the same reward (descend 0.72 against 0.07), the opposite of the toy,
  where flat shaped RL was best. The arm task has a longer chain and a narrower grasp.
- Lifting from scratch is not solved. Grasping is the stage that stalls (strict two-sided grasp).
- I killed both v2 runs by mistake at 6 of 7 M steps (a cleanup command for the toy matched their workers); their
  curves to 6 M are saved under `rg_arm_20261122/v2_runs`.
- The gate holds the whole command when one joint is out of bounds; the harness clips into the bounds first, or random
  exploration freezes the arm (40 to 50% of actions held).

## What this means for training from scratch in the arm sim

- Reaching and descending are learnable from scratch in 2 to 3 M steps (about 1.5 hours on this machine).
- Build the ladder as prefixes under one potential; add each new step as a stage that starts where the last ends.
- Keep outputs unsaturated (bounded mean, small penalty before the squash), above all the gripper.
- Replay old stages success-only.
- Train the low stages (reach, descend, grasp) over the whole workspace and physics range, and the expensive high
  stages narrowly; test whether they inherit. That is the experiment worth a day.
- Vision is not in any of this. From-scratch RL from pixels at 500 steps per second is out of reach here; the route
  is state-input RL teachers distilled into the vision policy.

## Limits

- The toy is a point mass with exact state; the inheritance result has not been tested in the arm sim.
- 5 seeds for most rows, 10 for round 8; staged recipes are bimodal (a seed learns the task fully or not at all).
- The four conditions were found one after another on the same toy; each fix was tested, not each combination.
- Arm sim: one seed per run.

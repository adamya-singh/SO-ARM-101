        Why our CNN looks so different from theirs

        Who does the seeing

        They hand-wrote the perception: one orange ball against a plain paddle, so a colour rule finds it every time. Our cube and square can be anywhere on the bench, under randomized lighting and colours, so we make the network learn to see. That is why our first half is a stack of conv layers and theirs is a threshold.

        What the network has to output

        Their controller outputs one small correction per frame. Ours imitates a teacher that plans a whole reach, so it outputs a 90-step plan, 540 numbers, from a single look. A bigger output needs a bigger head.

        How it was trained

        Theirs is reinforcement learning. It tries, fails and learns to recover, which only works with a fast loop. Ours copies a perfect scripted teacher, so it never sees a mistake and never learns to correct one. That's why looking more often has helped us less than it helps them.

        The difference that matters

        Our ACT is roughly a thousand times larger than anything their balancer needs, and both of our policies do use the camera: blank the images and each one drops from 21/30 to 0/30, missing the cube by about 11 cm. Their advantage is in the loop, not the network. They turn pixels into four clean numbers with a colour threshold, and they act on fresh information 30 times a second. Our policies read raw pixels and commit to a 1–3 second plan between looks, so a few millimetres of joint or calibration error have time to become a miss.
